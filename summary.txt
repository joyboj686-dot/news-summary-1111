World news, Wednesday 11 November 2026.
Ukraine: US-mediated talks in Abu Dhabi ended with Russia and Ukraine agreeing a large prisoner swap, AP and Al Jazeera report. US envoy Kushner called it the war's biggest humanitarian release. No ceasefire or land deal. He and Witkoff brief Putin on 12 Nov, then Zelenskyy.
Hormuz: Revolutionary Guards said tankers hit mines in the strait, and an ADNOC vessel was damaged in UAE waters on 10 Nov (Reuters, AP). At least 10 ships were hit in early November after 37 attacks in October (Washington Institute). Iran says the strait stays shut until the US answers its seven conditions.
COP31: At Antalya, UN chief Guterres warned the climate crisis is outrunning us and urged unabated coal phased out by 2040 (Al Jazeera, AP, UN News). Leaders of the US, China and India skipped it; UK PM Andy Burnham was the only G7 leader present.
US economy: October CPI rose 0.4% month on month versus 0.3% expected, core 0.3%, with no annual rate because of last year's shutdown (BLS via Reuters). The S&P 500 fell 0.38% to 7,521; markets see a December Fed rate rise as near-certain.
Nvidia: Shares fell 5.5% on 10 Nov to $168.89 as CEO Jensen Huang faces an internal probe over alleged links to a Chinese spy (Reuters, NDTV).
US politics: The House voted 211-204 to refer Thomas Massie's impeachment articles against Pete Hegseth to committee, effectively killing them; Jim Jordan became Republican minority leader (CBS, AP).
Ebola: DR Congo reports 11,042 cases and 5,387 deaths, the second-largest outbreak on record (Reuters).
Spying: A US Army soldier at NATO's Allied Rapid Reaction Corps HQ was arrested on suspicion of spying for Russia; the Kremlin denies it (ABC, CBS).
Gaza: Israeli strikes killed at least seven, including a father and his 13-year-old son, as the truce frays (Just Security).
South Korea: A bus hit 32 vehicles in Seongnam, injuring 57; the driver blamed brake failure (Yonhap, Reuters).
